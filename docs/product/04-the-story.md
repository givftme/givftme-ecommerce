## The long version


### It started with a picture book


Growing up, when visitors came to the house, we brought out the picture books.

That was the ritual. Somebody would sit down, and we would put the album in their hands and turn the pages with them. This was that Christmas. This was the day of that wedding. This was so-and-so before he travelled. We were not showing off photographs. We were showing them us. Here is who we are. Here is what we have lived through together.

It took me years to understand that the picture book was itself a gift.

Somebody bought the camera. Somebody paid for the prints when printing cost money. Somebody sat down on an ordinary evening, sorted the photographs, and arranged them in order so that one day we would be able to go back. Nobody announced it. Nobody called it a present. But that is exactly what it was, and it may be the most generous one I have ever received.

Because that is what a photograph does. It stops time. It takes a day that would otherwise soften at the edges and it holds it still, so that twenty years later you can pick it up and stand inside it again. Memory is a gift and I do not think we thank God for it enough, but memory on its own does not stay sharp. A photograph gives it something to hold on to.

That is the whole idea, and it was there from the beginning. A gift is somebody deciding, on purpose, that you should be able to keep this.

### Then we found the same thing everywhere


Once you understand what the picture book was doing, you start seeing it in everything people give you.

A shirt my parents bought me. A pair of shoes. A card a friend wrote on my birthday, with my name on it in his own handwriting. A mug. A frame somebody gave me and never explained, because it did not need explaining.

That frame is the whole thing in one object. A gift whose only purpose is to hold a memory. Somebody gives you a frame and what they are really saying is: there is a moment in your life worth putting on a wall, and I want you to look at it every day.

None of those things were expensive. That was never the point.

The point is what happens to an object once somebody gives it to you. A mug stops being a mug. It becomes the person who handed it over, and the afternoon it happened, and the version of you that received it. You can pick it up ten years later and the whole day comes back.

The picture book was the first one. Everything else joined it.

### And then we started losing people


Time passes and you lose friends. You lose people you love.

And when that happens, everything they ever gave you changes weight. The photograph in the album is no longer a photograph. The card in the drawer is no longer a card. The shirt is no longer a shirt. Each one is physical proof that they were here, that they thought about you, and that at some point they decided you should be able to keep this.

You find yourself going through your things looking for them. And sometimes you find something and you hold it and you are grateful in a way that is hard to explain.

And sometimes there is nothing. And that is its own kind of grief.

### The watch I never bought


There was a man I loved and respected deeply. A mentor. One of those people who shapes you without ever announcing that they are doing it.

I told him, more than once, that I was going to get him a watch.

I meant it every time I said it. I just kept pushing it forward. Next month. After this project. When things settle. There was always a reason, and none of the reasons were real reasons.

Then he died.

And the door closed. Not for a while. For good. There is no version of the future where I get to give him that watch. That opportunity is gone, permanently, and it is gone because of me.

That pain has not left me. I do not think it is supposed to. It taught me something I needed to learn, and it taught me in the only way I would have listened.

Here is what it taught me. We say we value people. But value is not a feeling. Value is something you do, and it has a deadline. Love that waits for a convenient moment is not being stored up. It is being lost.

### That is where Givtme comes from


Givtme is not an idea I had in a meeting. It is the thing I wish had existed before I lost my chance.

It exists for one reason: so that the people who love somebody never have to find out too late that they left it too long.

Somebody sat down once and made us a picture book. We are trying to make it easier for everybody else to do the same kind of thing, for the people they love, while there is still time.

## What Givtme actually does


Three things. They are not features. They are the three places where love usually gets dropped.

### First, it remembers


You should not have to carry every date in your head. Nobody can. That is why we forget, and then we feel terrible about forgetting, and then we send money on the day with an apology attached, and the moment passes without meaning.

So you tell Givtme once. Your mother's birthday. Your friend's anniversary. The naming ceremony. The day your brother finally leaves for the airport.

Then you put your phone down and go and live your life.

And Givtme carries it for you. Thirty days before, you hear from us quietly, so there is time to plan something proper. Seven days before, we come back with real suggestions, not a bare alert. The day before, if you have not moved, we make it one tap.

You will not forget. That is the whole promise. You will never again have to say I meant to.

### Second, it lets you say what you want


Here is something we do not talk about enough. Receiving is awkward too.

People who love you want to celebrate you. They genuinely do. But they are guessing, and they are afraid of guessing wrong, and so they buy something safe and forgettable, and you say thank you and put it in a cupboard, and everybody involved loses something.

A wishlist fixes that without anybody having to be rude about it. You are not demanding anything. You are making it easy for people who already want to show up for you.

Put anything on it. Things from our store. Things you saw somewhere else and paste in. Things you have wanted for a year and never said out loud.

Then share the link, and let the people who love you stop guessing.

And when six friends want to give you one big thing instead of six small ones, they can put it together. Nobody has to be embarrassed about the size of what they can give. Everybody gets to be part of it.

### Third, it helps you when you have no idea


There is a specific kind of paralysis that comes with caring about someone.

You want to get it right, and because you want to get it right you do not know where to start, and because you do not know where to start you do it late, and because you do it late you do it badly.

So we built a place to go when your mind is blank. Not a catalogue. A collection of what people are actually asking to be remembered by, organised by relationship and by feeling rather than by category. For a mother who says she does not need anything. For the friend who has had a hard year. For somebody who is leaving.

You will find something. You are not starting from nothing anymore.

## What we are really doing


We are not in the business of selling things to people.

We are in the business of making sure the picture gets framed. That the card gets written. That the watch gets given while there is still somebody there to receive it.

Because one day, somebody will go through their things and find something you gave them, and stop, and hold it, and stand inside that day again. That is the same work the picture book was doing on all those evenings when the visitors came. Holding a moment still. Keeping a person close.

That is what we are protecting.

Africans have always known this. We are a people who give. It is in how we celebrate, how we mourn, how we welcome a child, how we send somebody off. We have never needed to be taught that love is something you carry to someone's door.

We are just making sure it arrives on time.
